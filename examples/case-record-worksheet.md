# Case Record Worksheet (score-free)

A paper companion to `case-record-template.json`. Fill it in on your own system. Nothing on this sheet produces a number, a band, or a rank.

> **Limitations and intended use. Read before filling in.**
>
> - **Not predictive.** The vocabulary describes behaviors that have been reported in past cases. It does not predict violence. Showing one of these behaviors does not mean a person will harm anyone.
> - **Type-level vocabulary.** An indicator describes a kind of behavior. Tagging an observation with an indicator says only that the wording fits what was reported. It says nothing about the person.
> - **Not a risk score.** This record holds no score, band, weight, or priority, and none should be added to it.
> - **No adverse action on the record alone.** This record is not grounds for discipline, dismissal, exclusion, or any other adverse action by itself. Such decisions need their own process, facts, and advice.
> - **Individualized assessment.** Each person is assessed on the facts of their own case by a trained team. Do not reason from the vocabulary to the person.
> - **Protective factors and context.** Look for protective factors and benign explanations every time, and write down what you found, including when you found none.
> - **Document sources.** For every observation, write where it came from and whether it has been corroborated.
>
> The individual is called the **person of concern** throughout. The vocabulary's indicator text uses the label "threat actor" for the class of behavior it describes. Do not copy that label into a record about someone who has not been assessed as posing a threat.

## 3. The six instance factors

These describe the case as it stands at the latest entry. They are prompts for what to find out and a tidy way to write down what is known. They are not added up. **`unknown` is always an acceptable answer and never counts for or against the person of concern.** Check one per factor and write the facts behind each in the basis box.

**Target focus.** How far is the behavior directed at a target?
☐ diffuse (no particular target) ☐ narrowing (a set of candidates, or a place associated with them) ☐ specific_target (one person, group, or site) ☐ unknown

**Pathway stage.** Where does the observed behavior sit on the pathway to violence? The stage names are adapted from the pathway to intended violence described by Calhoun and Weston.
☐ grievance ☐ ideation ☐ research_planning ☐ preparation ☐ breach_or_probing ☐ attack ☐ aftermath ☐ unknown
*There is no "not on the pathway" box. If the behavior does not place the person on the pathway, check unknown and say so in the basis.*

**Means in hand.** Does the person of concern actually possess, or have they acquired, the means now?
☐ unknown ☐ assessed_absent ☐ partial ☐ assessed_present ☐ confirmed_present
- **assessed_absent:** the team judges, on stated information, that the means are not held.
- **partial:** some but not all of the means are held.
- **assessed_present:** the team judges the means are held, on credible information, though no one on the team has seen them.
- **confirmed_present:** the means were directly observed or recovered.
- **unknown:** no information. This is the default.

*This factor is about means actually possessed or acquired. **Lawful ability to acquire something is not means in hand.** Many adults could lawfully acquire something harmful; that fact says nothing about this person and must not be recorded here.*

**Tempo trajectory.** Across the observations, is the behavior speeding up, holding, or dropping off?
☐ accelerating ☐ steady ☐ stalling ☐ unknown

**Proximity and access.** What access does the person have to the target? This is a state, not a behavior: a coworker has access without ever seeking it, and having access is a fact about the setting.
☐ no_known_access ☐ seeking_access ☐ has_access ☐ at_or_near_target ☐ unknown

**Source credibility.** How reliable is the reporting behind this record? This describes the reporting, not the person.
☐ high ☐ moderate ☐ low ☐ unknown

## 4. Protective factors and benign explanations

Required. Work through the prompts and write down what you found. If you looked and found nothing, write that you looked.

- Is there an ordinary explanation for the behavior? Has anyone checked it?
- Is the person using a legitimate channel for the grievance (appeal, complaint process, representative, counsel)?
- What steady ties does the person have: family, friends, work, community, faith, treatment or support they have chosen?
- What does the person stand to lose, and do they speak about the future?
- How has the person responded to limits, feedback, or contact from the team?
- Is there a history of handling conflict without threats or violence?
- Did the person explain, retract, or apologize when asked?

## 6. Reassessment

Reassess when new information arrives and at whatever interval your policy sets. Concern can go **up, down, or stay the same**. Each entry needs a reason. When concern changes, update the factor values in section 3 to the current picture and keep the earlier picture in the rationale, so the record shows how the team's view moved.
