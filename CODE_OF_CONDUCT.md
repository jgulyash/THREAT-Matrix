# Code of Conduct

This applies to everyone who takes part in the THREAT Matrix project, in every project space: issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and any other channel the project uses.

## What is expected

- Be civil. Criticize ideas, wording, and evidence, not people.
- Assume good faith, and say so plainly when you disagree.
- Back claims with public sources. Accept correction when a source does not say what you thought it said.
- Accept a declined contribution as a judgment about the contribution. Ask what would make it acceptable, or try a different approach.
- Disclose a conflict of interest when you have one, as [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) section 2.3 describes.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, threats, insults, or demeaning comments, including about a person's identity, background, beliefs, or health.
- Sexual language or unwelcome personal attention.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Sustained disruption, or arguing in bad faith.
- Impersonating a person or organization, or claiming an endorsement that has not been given.

## Two rules specific to this project

Because this project describes behavior relevant to threat assessment, two rules apply in every project space, whatever the intent:

1. **Do not post details of real cases or identifiable people.** Use fictionalized examples or cite published public sources.
2. **Do not use project spaces to assess, label, or speculate about any individual.** That includes other participants.

## Reporting

Report a conduct concern privately, through the private channel described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), and say that your message is a conduct report. Do not open a public issue that names the person concerned or repeats the material complained of.

A report should say what happened, where, and when, with links if they exist.

## What happens next

The maintainer reads every report and replies when a reply is possible. Depending on what happened, the response may be a private request to stop, removal or editing of the content, a temporary restriction, or a permanent ban from project spaces. The reporter's identity is not disclosed without their consent.

The project has one maintainer today. If a report concerns the maintainer, send it through the same private channel and say so. There is not yet anyone independent to receive it, and the record of the report is kept so that a reviewer or review board member can examine it once one is seated ([GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) section 2). This is a known gap.
